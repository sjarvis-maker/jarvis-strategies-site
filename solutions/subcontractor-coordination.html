Streamline Subcontractor Coordination

If you run a GC or a trades business that coordinates other trades, the time goes to choreography, not craft. A practical AI workflow strips out the friction around capturing and propagating information, while the relationship work stays with your people.

The problem

The work happens. People know how to swing hammers, pull wire, run pipe. What eats the day is the choreography: who's on site when, what got finished yesterday so the next trade can start today, what the electrician needs from the framer before they show up, what changed about the schedule because of a delivery delay, who's clearing the rough-in before the inspector arrives.

That coordination work is mostly communication: phone calls, texts, emails, the site group chat, the project management software nobody fully fills in. It eats hours a day for the PM and the super.

Where AI genuinely helps

  • Daily site summaries. A PM gets fifteen status updates a day from different trades, mostly via text or quick calls. AI reads those, extracts what changed, and produces a one-paragraph summary pushed to everyone who needs it. Time saved: about 45 minutes a day, every day.
  • RFI triage. RFIs come in unstructured and sit in inboxes. AI categorizes incoming RFIs by trade, urgency, and which drawing they reference, then routes them to the right person with the right context. Time saved per project: hours.
  • Schedule impact analysis. When something slips, figuring out the downstream impact across trades is normally a 30-minute exercise. AI flags affected dependencies in under a minute. The PM still makes the call.

Where it goes sideways

The temptation is to let AI talk directly to subs. Don't. The relationship between a GC and its subs is the part of the job that matters most, and subs need to know there's a human on the other end of the message. Letting AI auto-send schedule updates is how you train your subs to ignore your project communications. Use AI for the back-office work. Keep humans on the front line.

Governance

Every time AI summarizes communications, something might get missed: a dependency, a safety incident, an urgent RFI miscategorized as routine. Treat AI output as supplemental rather than authoritative. The full message thread stays the source of truth, the summary is a faster way to get the gist, and the PM still looks at the underlying data when something's high-stakes. AI for first-pass and acceleration, humans for the decisions that matter.

When to look at this

If you're a PM or super spending more than two hours a day in project management software and text threads, the workflow is probably worth examining. The question to ask: where in your coordination work is the rote part, and what would it free up if it took 80% less time? That's what the assessment surfaces.
